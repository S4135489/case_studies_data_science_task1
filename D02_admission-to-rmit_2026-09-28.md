# Admission to RMIT

RMIT admits students from a range of educational backgrounds, including completion of Year 12 results, previous higher education (HE) or vocational education training (VET) study, work and life experience, and for some programs interviews, auditions or portfolios.

RMIT is committed to providing transparency to the admissions process, to help applicants understand and make informed choices about applying to their chosen program (course).

## Admission criteria

### Principles of selection

To meet program eligibility, all applicants to RMIT are assessed based on the minimum academic entry requirements published on the RMIT program (course) page and the VTAC CourseSearch.

All applicants must meet the minimum academic entry requirements for the program (course). Applicants with overseas qualifications will be assessed for Australian equivalence.

**Meeting these requirements does not guarantee entry into the program (course)** due to the competitive nature of the program (course).

Where a program (course) requires a selection task, this is outlined on the program page and VTAC CourseSearch.

Eligible applicants who have faced educational, financial and other disadvantages which may have impacted their most recent academic performance can apply for consideration through RMIT's equity access schemes.

### Applicants with recent secondary education (within the past two years)

Applicants who are currently completing an Australian Year 12 (or equivalent), or International Baccalaureate (IB), or who have completed an Australian Year 12 (or equivalent) in the last two years, must meet the published minimum admissions criteria for the program, including any program prerequisites.

Some programs also consider ATAR as the basis of selection; applicants can view the previous year's ATAR profile as a guide.

### Applicants with vocational education and training (VET) study

Applicants must have satisfactorily completed an accredited award at certificate IV or higher as published on the program page. Applicants will be assigned an equivalent selection rank (comparable to an ATAR) based on the AQF level of their highest VET qualification completed.

### Applicants with higher education (HE) study

Applicants must have satisfactorily completed, on an award or non-award basis at any university (including Open Universities Australia), at least two courses (subjects) at undergraduate level equivalent in volume of learning to at least 24 RMIT University credit points, taught and assessed entirely in English, unless stated otherwise on the program page.

Applicants will be assigned an equivalent selection rank based on their GPA.

### Applicants with work and life experience

Applicants who left secondary education more than two years previously and have not undertaken VET or HE study since then must meet the published minimum admissions criteria for the program, including any program prerequisites.

### Skills for Tertiary Admissions Test (STAT)

Where an applicant has no prior Year 12, VET qualifications, HE studies or relevant work experience that meets the program's minimum entry requirements, and is 19 years of age or over (as of 1 January) and has not enrolled in secondary studies in the last two years, they may sit the STAT Multiple Choice and achieve the required minimum score to satisfy the Year 12 English language prerequisite:

- Bachelor degree: overall score of at least 156 or verbal reasoning sub-score of 155
- Associate degree: overall score of at least 150 or verbal reasoning sub-score of 149

Where the program states that an applicant may sit the STAT, the results will be converted to an equivalent ATAR and used for selection.

### Under 17 applicants – Transition from School

Direct applicants who are under 17 years of age and have not completed Year 12, but meet the program's entry requirements, will be made a conditional offer, requiring correspondence from the School Principal or Regional Director confirming an exemption from attending secondary school.

### Australian Tertiary Admission Rank (ATAR)

ATAR is a rank between 0.00 (lowest) and 99.95 (highest) that measures an individual's overall academic achievement for the VCE relative to other students who graduated in the same year, used for making selection decisions.

### Selection rank

Selection rank is the ranking used to determine an applicant's admission to a program. It can include ATAR, adjustments (equity, subject), contributions from work experience or previous study, personal statement, portfolio assessments, STAT results or other supplementary tests.

### Personal statement

A personal statement may (depending on the program): provide an adjustment to the selection rank for non-Year 12 applicants (up to 20 points); be used as a scored/ranked selection instrument; be required to demonstrate suitability (with resume, references or interview); or be encouraged for applicants excluded from a program due to academic progress, outlining strategies for future success.

### Prerequisites

A prerequisite is study an applicant must have completed at secondary school or equivalent to be eligible for a particular program, e.g. Bachelor of Mechanical Engineering requires VCE Units 3 and 4 English and Mathematical Methods with a study score of 25 or equivalent. Prerequisites are specific to each program and found on the program page or VTAC CourseSearch.

### Guaranteed Entry ATAR

Select RMIT programs have a Guaranteed ATAR. Applicants who receive an ATAR equal to or above this and meet course prerequisites are guaranteed an offer. Applicants below the guarantee may still receive an offer depending on adjustment factors and place availability. For VTAC applicants, the program must be their highest eligible preference.

## Adjustment Factors

Applicants may be eligible for adjustment factors (additional points combined with ATAR), up to a maximum of 28 aggregate adjustment points across categories including:

- Equity access schemes
- VTAC Equity schemes & scholarships and RMIT Access (max 20 aggregate points)
- Regional Adjustment Scheme (automatic, based on low socio-economic status area)
- Schools Network Access Program (SNAP), for designated Victorian secondary schools
- Indigenous Access Program (also: 5 points lower minimum English study score for bachelor degree applicants)
- Year 12 Subject Adjustments (cumulative, max 8 aggregate points)
- Elite Athlete and Performer Adjustment (REAP): 10 aggregate adjustment factors for VET competitive entry, associate degree or undergraduate degree courses

## How to apply

Applicants currently undertaking an Australian Year 12 (or equivalent) in Australia or overseas should apply through VTAC.

All other applicants can apply direct to RMIT, provided they have not commenced a VTAC application for a program listed as available through VTAC. Otherwise, applications should be made through VTAC.

For programs not offered through VTAC, all applicants can apply direct to RMIT.

International applicants should follow the international student "how to apply" guide.

## Enrolment

Once an applicant receives an offer, they must accept and enrol. Direct applicants log in to accept via their RMIT dashboard. VTAC applicants receive an email from VTAC notifying them of their offer, and RMIT confirms via the VTAC nominated email with enrolment instructions.

Applicants who are not ready to commence can choose to defer their studies.

## Formal review of selection decision

An applicant dissatisfied with the outcome of their application for admission must contact the relevant college admissions office for feedback **within 10 working days** from the day formal notification of the selection decision was sent, before submitting a formal review of a selection decision, in accordance with RMIT's Selection Procedure.

- College of Business and Law
- College of Design and Social Context
- STEM College
- College of Vocational Education

## Student Profile 2026

Indicative peer cohort for new students at RMIT, commencing undergraduate study Semester 1 2026 and enrolled past the census date, across all Australian campuses and international students studying in Australia:

- Higher education study: 1,829 students (14.10%)
- Vocational education (VET) study: 1,283 students (9.89%)
- Work and life experience: 730 students (5.63%)
- Recent secondary education: 7,161 students (55.21%) — of which admitted on ATAR alone 6,396 (49.31%), admitted on both ATAR and additional criteria 257 (0.19%), admitted on other criteria only 40 (5.71%; figures as published)
- International students: 1,967 students (15.17%)
- All students: 12,970 (100%)
